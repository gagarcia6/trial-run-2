ManCHILD

A Reflective Essay on the Influence of Technical Misogyny on Teen Boys

In the age of social media and artificial intelligence, the irresponsible misuse of technology has become both fast and widespread. Recently, this institution faced a serious issue involving AI-generated deepfakes of young female students created without consent. This is not a prank nor a harmless joke, but a direct violation of human dignity and personal safety. It cannot be excused under the phrase “boys will be boys.”

Such behavior does not exist in isolation. Young boys are exposed daily to misogynistic content through short-form videos and online personalities that repackage dominance, emotional detachment, and aggression as confidence. The “manosphere,” a growing network of online communities, promotes a rigid hierarchy of masculinity where boys are taught that a “real man” dominates rather than understands, detaches rather than empathizes.

What is especially dangerous is how misogyny is framed as truth. Cruelty is rebranded as “honesty” and entitlement as “self-improvement.” Through repetition and algorithmic reinforcement, these ideas stop appearing extreme and become normalized. In this environment, immaturity is not only excused but rewarded. Weaponized incompetence, emotional illiteracy, and lack of accountability are seen as masculine strengths. The idea of “real men” becomes ironic, as what is encouraged is not growth or responsibility, but entitlement and arrested development. These boys are not empowered; they are stunted.

Netflix's Adolescence reflects this process, showing how misogyny is gradually shaped through peer validation, online influence, and silence from authority figures. The show illustrates how boys learn early that cruelty can earn respect, that humiliation can be entertainment, and that accountability is optional. Misogyny becomes a social language, reinforced by laughter, likes, and the absence of concrete consequences. This is patriarchy in practice: a system that teaches boys power without responsibility.

When such mindsets are paired with accessible, powerful technology, artificial intelligence becomes a weapon for exploitation rather than a tool for progress. Harm is detached from responsibility, and violations are reduced to digital “content.” The distance of a screen allows cruelty to feel abstract and run rampant, even when its consequences are deeply personal and permanent. It is deeply unsettling how easily misogynistic content is accepted as the truth by so many young boys. If the normalization of women-hating continues unchecked, society risks moving backwards while pretending to move forward.

As a young female student in this current digital age, I believe technology must be guided by a clear conscience. Not everything that can be done should be done, especially when it will result in harm. True progress should be measured not only by innovation, but by empathy, responsibility, and moral growth as well. Without these, technology does not lead us forward; it simply amplifies our worst failures.

BY Gytha Garcia, 10 - Talisay
